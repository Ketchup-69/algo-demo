## The objection is never capability

Ask a finance director whether software could match a purchase order to a goods
received note and an invoice, and nobody argues. The reconciliation itself is
rules-based work. It has been mechanisable for a long time.

The objection is about the exception. What happens on the line that does not
reconcile, when the system has to decide whether a quantity variance is a
delivery shortfall, a unit-of-measure mismatch, or a supplier error.

## Draft, do not post

The design answer is to keep the agent on the preparation side of the line.
It collects the documents, does the reconciliation, drafts the action, and
shows its working — including what it could not resolve and why.

A person makes the decision that changes the system of record.

That sounds like a limitation. In practice it is the thing that makes the
deployment approvable at all: the control that already exists in the process
does not have to be rewritten, because it has not been removed.
